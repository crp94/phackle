## GR2a — the playthrough table

Three player models, 32 consecutive days each, fresh storage per model.
*Naive knob-turner*: one random knob per move, publishes the instant the dial
drops under .05, believes what it published. *Greedy hacker*: hill-climbs the
visible p-value, one knob at a time (tails → outcome → subgroup → exclusion →
transform → covariates), buys more data when a full sweep stalls. *Informed
caller*: hacks identically, then probes 8 random sibling specs **inside the
published spec's own outcome family** and calls "real" iff ≥60% of the probes
are significant — §2.7.6's own lesson, computed from what a player can actually
see rather than from the oracle's 448.

| metric | naive knob-turner | greedy hacker | informed caller | §3.9 baseline |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **taps to first p < .05** (median) | **16.5** (mean 19.4, max 56; **never** on 8/32 days) | **3.0** (mean 8.0, max 31; never on 1/32) | **3.0** (same search) | band (b) = **11** (daily mix) |
| **session length** (proxy, median day) | **2.3 min** (tail 3.1 min at 56 turns) | **1.7 min** | **1.9 min** | bar: < 3 min |
| **call accuracy** | **0.500** (sens 1.000 · spec 0.333) | **0.281** (sens 1.000 · spec 0.042) | **0.875** (sens 0.500 · spec 1.000) | base rate 0.750; band (d) oracle 0.822 |
| **REPLICATED rate / 32 days** (longest drought) | 7 = 22% (7 d) | 6 = 19% (7 d) | 6 = 19% (7 d) | — |
| **week-one achievement unlocks** | **14** | **10** | **21** | bar: ≥ 2 |
| median forks · median k explored | 27.5 · 27.5 | 4.0 · 4 | 13.0 · 12 | — |
| publish rate · median score | 0.750 · 50 | 0.969 · **0** | 0.969 · 100 |  |
| parsimony bonus actually earned | 7/32 days | 8/32 days | **2/32 days** | — |

Session-length proxy = 12 s briefing + (turns × (0.365 s machine floor + 1.2 s
human dwell)) + 12 s Published + 55 s reveal read/scroll + 3 s call + 12 s
summary. The machine floor itself is measured, not assumed: briefing→lab with a
first result **435 ms**, one settled knob turn **589 ms**, submit→Published
**63 ms**, call→reveal **711 ms**, reveal→summary **289 ms**; a full 18-turn day
at machine speed is **8.07 s**.

**Honest-player REPLICATED rate: 0/30, structurally.** `verdictStamp()` returns
`NULL_REPORTED` whenever `published === null`, so a Hacking-Mode player who
takes the integrity path can never see the green stamp — on any day, ever. See
`[gr2-009]`.

**Where the bands and the felt experience disagree:** band (b) is measured on a
uniform random permutation of all 1,792 specs. The Lab offers exactly one move —
turn one knob — and that walk is a *correlated local* search, not a uniform
sample. Measured over 60 days × 20 walks each: uniform permutation median first
hit **10**, single-knob walk median **13**, and the walk **never finds anything
in 60 moves on 19.3% of walks** versus 7.6% for the permutation. Meanwhile the
greedy hill-climb — the move a competent player actually makes — lands in **3**.
The band is green and correct, and it does not describe either player.

## Findings

### [gr2-001] A preregistered analysis is a false positive ~28% of the time, and the game says "about 5%"

**Severity:** blocker (`scientific`) · **Effort:** L · **Where:** `src/engine/dgp.ts` + `src/engine/dgpConstants.ts` (the DGP), surfaced at `src/content/en/copy.ts:703` (`reveal.preregFalsePositive`), `src/game/scoring.ts:113` (`preregSigNull`), `src/ui/screens/Stats.tsx` (the α-made-visible panel)

**Evidence.** On RAW, unconditioned null datasets (no §3.3 acceptance loop), the
single simplest analysis — all participants, no covariates, no exclusions, raw
scale — rejects at α = .05 far more often than α:

    outcome  tails   N=200   N=400   mean beta   mean |t|   (500 draws; a true null gives 0.050 and E|t|≈0.80)
    Y1       two     0.176   0.286      0.1813   1.504
    Y1       one     0.270   0.402      0.1813   1.504
    Y2       two     0.132   0.138      0.0532   1.080
    Y3       two     0.218   0.366      0.2195   1.663
    Y3       one     0.314   0.494      0.2195   1.663
    Y4       two     0.052   0.048      0.0442   0.802   <-- the only calibrated column

On **accepted** null days (what a player is served): default spec @400 → **0.280**.
Mechanism: `TREATMENT_L1_COEF`/`TREATMENT_L4_COEF` build the treatment from latent
factors L1 and L4, and `Y1_LOADINGS`/`Y3_LOADINGS` load on those same factors —
so X and Y are *structurally* associated on a "null" day. That is honest
observational confounding and defensible as a DGP; what is not defensible is the
game telling the player otherwise.

Live, from the production build (prereg day, puzzle #5): the screen shows a red
**RETRACTED** stamp, the subline "The journal has issued a correction. This page
is the correction.", and then

> "This is not a mistake: a preregistered analysis, run exactly once, still finds
> a false positive about 5% of the time. Today was one of those days."

and the invoice reads `False positive — 0`. The reassurance is off by 5–10×, on
the one path the game recommends, in the one place it states α out loud.

**Game consequence, not just a numeric one.** Prereg Mode is the cure. Measured
over 60 days committing the default spec: score distribution `0:13 · 40:9 ·
100:32 · 150:6`, i.e. **22% of honest days score zero**, and the Stats screen's
"success rate" juxtaposition will read hack ≈ 97% vs prereg ≈ 29% instead of
≈100% vs ≈5% — the punchline loses most of its force. Worse, the incentive
inverts: preregistering the most defensible outcome (Y1, the default) is
punished 6× more than preregistering Y4, so the skill Prereg Mode rewards is
"know which column the engine left uncorrelated."

**Fix shape (controller decision — touches the DGP, an inviolable).** Three
options, in increasing cost: (a) keep the DGP and make every claim conditional
and true — the reveal's null truth line becomes "no *causal* effect; the
association you found is confounding, not chance", `reveal.preregFalsePositive`
quotes the measured rate, and the About page discloses the shared-latent
construction (cheapest, and arguably *better pedagogy* — confounding is a real
lesson); (b) orthogonalise X against the latent factors that feed Y1–Y3 so a
null day is a true null and every existing string becomes true (changes every
golden and the p_hit table); (c) leave it and accept a false statement in the
product. **This does not touch `tuning.ts`, so no calibration band moves** — I
re-ran `npm run cal` on this tree and all five bands PASS unchanged
(a 1.000 · b 11 · c 0.826 · d 0.822 · e 5).

Overlaps GR1a's A2 sweep by construction; deduplicate at synthesis.

### [gr2-002] On effect days the reveal calls the true effect's own paths "chance alone"

**Severity:** blocker (`scientific`) · **Effort:** S · **Where:** `src/content/en/copy.ts` (`reveal.accounting1`), rendered unconditionally at `src/ui/screens/Reveal.tsx:211`

**Evidence.** Production build, puzzle #8 (an effect day), consecutive lines on
one screen:

> True effect on **Items retrieved without unpacking (items/trip): β = 0.29**. On every other outcome, nothing.
> …
> Of **1792** possible analyses, **455 (25.4%)** reach p < .05 **by chance alone**.

455 of 1,792 paths are significant *because there is a real effect*, not by
chance. The sentence is `dayType`-blind. Measured across the calibration run,
effect days carry a median of **335.5** significant paths versus **85** on null
days — so this sentence is wrong by a factor of four on exactly the days it is
loudest.

**Fix shape.** Split the key: `reveal.accounting1Null` keeps "by chance alone";
`reveal.accounting1Effect` says something like "…reach p < .05 — most of them
because the effect is real, some of them not." Requires the IT/ES transcreation
of one new string. No engine change.

### [gr2-004] A finished day can be replayed forever, and the replay is silently thrown away

**Severity:** blocker · **Effort:** M · **Where:** `src/ui/screens/Briefing.tsx:63` (`showChooser = unlocked && !preregPlayedToday`), `src/ui/screens/Summary.tsx:375` (`alreadySaved`)

**Evidence.** Production build. Honest day on puzzle #5: abandon → call →
reveal → summary (`Integrity bonus 80`, `Score: 80`, share string emitted).
Reload:

    chooser present      : no
    cta                  : "OPEN THE DATA" enabled=true
    REPLAYED the same day: Lab reopened at p = 0.147 — the day the player already finished

The one-play-per-day guard lives **only** inside the mode chooser, and the
chooser only exists once `first_retraction` is unlocked. So the guard is absent
for (a) every player on day one, and (b) permanently for the honest player, who
by definition never publishes and therefore never earns a retraction. The
replayed day reaches Published, the CALL, a *different* verdict experience, and
a Summary whose `saveDay`/`saveAchievements` are skipped by `alreadySaved` —
with no message. The player is shown a score, a streak line and a share string
that were never recorded.

For contrast, the guarded path works: after publishing on puzzle #5 and
reloading, `PLAY HACKING MODE` is `enabled=false` with "Already played today",
and storage holds the correct single record.

**Fix shape.** Move the already-played check out of the chooser and into the
Briefing itself: when `history[iso].hack` exists and prereg is not available,
render the day's finished state (the share string + the countdown) instead of
"Open the data", exactly as the chooser's disabled branch already does. A
"one play per day" rule that only exists for players who cheat is the wrong way
round.

### [gr2-007] The parsimony bonus is dead for anyone who plays the skill the game teaches

**Severity:** high · **Effort:** M (touches `tuning.ts` → controller) · **Where:** `src/game/tuning.ts:15` (`parsimonyMax: 40, parsimonyPerFork: 4`), `src/game/scoring.ts:71`

**Evidence.** The bonus is `max(0, 40 − 4 × forks)`, so it is zero at 10 forks.
Measured forks per day: greedy hacker median **4**, naive **27.5**, informed
caller **13**. The informed caller — the only model that beats the base rate —
earned any parsimony at all on **2 of 32 days**, because §2.7.6's own lesson
("robustness across specifications") *requires* spending forks to probe the
family. Live confirmation, puzzle #8: `Correct call 100 | Parsimony bonus 0`.

So §1.2 pillar 3 ("scoring rewards the real/noise call, parsimony, and restraint")
is internally contradictory: the call is worth 100, parsimony at most 40, and
buying accuracy costs parsimony. A rational player ignores parsimony entirely —
which is what my highest-scoring model does (informed total 2888 vs naive 2476
vs greedy 1264 over 32 days).

**Fix shape (controller, with band impact stated).** `SCORING.*` is **not** in
`reveal.ts#dgpConstantVector`, so no p_hit regeneration and **no calibration
band moves** — I re-ran `npm run cal` and all five bands PASS. Options:
(a) make parsimony count *distinct outcome families* touched rather than forks,
so family-probing is free and outcome-shopping is not; (b) raise
`parsimonyMax`/lower `parsimonyPerFork` so the honest range 10–15 forks still
pays something; (c) delete the row and stop claiming to reward parsimony. (a)
is the only one that makes the scoring agree with the pedagogy.

### [gr2-008] Optional stopping — the master spec's "crown jewel" — is dead content

**Severity:** high · **Effort:** M · **Where:** `src/ui/screens/Lab.tsx:140-151`, `src/game/tuning.ts` (`N_SCHEDULE`)

**Evidence.** §2.4 calls "Collect 50 more participants" the crown jewel and gives
it the game's only sanctioned Act-I wink (the Armitage footnote, which needs
**2** peeks). Across 32 days × 3 models the `one_more_batch` achievement (3+
peeks) unlocked **once** for the greedy model, **once** for the informed model
and **never** for the naive model. Reason: a player only presses it when stuck,
and hill-climbing on six knobs (1,792 paths, median 3 evaluations to a hit) is
almost never stuck. The Armitage line and the peek surcharge in the reveal are
therefore seen by a small minority of players — and the surcharge is one of the
three numbers the accounting paragraph exists to deliver.

**Fix shape.** Make peeking *attractive* rather than a fallback: it is the one
move that visibly improves an estimate's precision (n and CI both move), so
surface that — e.g. the CoefPlot's CI is the thing that narrows, and the button
sits under the dial where nothing suggests it does anything but "more data". Or
gate one of Act I's rewards on it. Do **not** solve it by making search harder.

### [gr2-009] The honest path has no positive moment, in any mode, ever

**Severity:** high · **Effort:** M (design) · **Where:** `src/engine/reveal.ts:81` (`verdictStamp`), `src/ui/screens/Summary.tsx`

**Evidence.** `verdictStamp` returns `NULL_REPORTED` for `published === null`
unconditionally, so a Hacking-Mode player who reports a null **cannot** reach
REPLICATED on an effect day — even though they were right, and even though the
game pays them for it (`Missed discovery +20` / `Integrity bonus +80`). Live,

    REVEAL stamp   : NULL REPORTED

Two glyphs, no ceremony, no stamp anyone would screenshot, and — because
`first_retraction` is what gates Prereg Mode — no progression either. The player

honest path, currently nothing.

Note the interaction with `[gr2-004]`: the honest player is also the one who can
replay the day forever, because both gates hang off the same unlock.

**Fix shape.** The stamp vocabulary already has room: an effect day where the

confirmed null — two distinct, nameable outcomes currently collapsed into one
grey NULL REPORTED. Give the honest correct call its own mark (§2.9's share
vocabulary would need one glyph, at most). Separately, unlock Prereg Mode on
*first completed day* rather than on first retraction, so integrity is not a
progression dead end.

### [gr2-012] The band measures a search the interface cannot perform

**Severity:** high · **Effort:** M (calibration definition → controller) · **Where:** `scripts/simulate_calibration.ts` (band b's explorer model), §3.9b

**Evidence.** Band (b) walks a *uniformly random permutation of all 1,792 specs*.
The Lab offers exactly one move: change one knob. Measured over 60 days ×
20 walks per model:

                              median first hit   P(hit≤5)  ≤10    ≤15    ≤20    ≤30    ≤60
    single-knob random walk        13             0.235   0.350  0.448  0.523  0.621  0.807
    uniform permutation            10             0.312   0.506  0.617  0.695  0.810  0.924

So the number the balance sheet certifies (11) sits between two experiences that

design, and that's the lesson". The sinking feeling the design wants is "I
cannot lose"; the one a naive player gets one day in five is "I cannot win".
Confirmed in the store playthroughs: the naive model reached p < .05 on only
**24 of 32 days** and hit its 60-turn ceiling on 8.

**Fix shape (controller).** Band (b) should be *reported for both explorers* —
the permutation (comparable to §3.9's history) and the single-knob walk (the
one the UI affords) — with the walk's own acceptable range pinned. This is a
change to the calibration suite's reporting, not to any constant, so **no band
value moves** (verified: `npm run cal` PASS ×5 on this tree). If the walk's
19.3% miss rate is judged unacceptable, the lever is `NULL_SIG_BAND`'s floor,
and the suite already prints that what-if table — raising the ceiling costs the
reveal's "N (x%) by chance alone" headline, which the controller declined on
2026-08-03 for good reason.

### [gr2-017] The reveal's headline statistic understates the best hackers

**Severity:** polish · **Effort:** M · **Where:** `src/engine/reveal.ts#pHitAtK`, `src/data/p_hit_by_k.json`, `reveal.accounting3`

**Evidence.** `pHit` is simulated on a **uniform random** explorer. The competent
player is not one: hill-climbing on the visible p-value reaches significance in
a median of **3–4** evaluations, and `pHit[4] = 0.181`. So on the modal
well-played day the accounting says

> "A researcher randomly exploring 4 of them finds at least one 'significant'
> result about 18% of the time."

which reads as *you were unlucky-lucky*, and lets an efficient hacker off. The
sentence is loudest and most damning for the *worst* player (naive, k = 27.5 →

name the difference between random exploration and *directed* exploration

significance far faster than this"). That is also the actual lesson of Gelman &
Loken's garden, which the current sentence half-tells. Alternative: add a
directed-search row to the calibration table and quote whichever is larger.
**No constant changes; no band impact.**

### [gr2-018] The most efficient hacker gets the dullest celebration

### [gr2-020] Owner question: should prereg days ever fire the hacking-satire achievements?

**Severity:** polish (verdict, no code change) · **Effort:** S · **Where:** `src/game/achievements.ts`

**Verdict: uphold wontfix — but close the gap it exposes.**

The seven hacking achievements are structurally unreachable in Prereg Mode and
should stay that way. `harking`, `one_tailed_bandit`, `outlier_surgeon`,
`subgroup_safari`, `one_more_batch` and `garden` are all citations for behaviour
that *cannot occur* under preregistration — there is no second spec to change
your mind toward, no result to see before flipping tails, no batch to collect.
Awarding "For hypothesizing after the results were known, and knowing it" to a
player who declared the hypothesis before seeing a number would be the game
lying about what happened, which is the one thing §1.2 pillar 4 forbids. And
`well_actually` ("you knew, and did it anyway") requires a CALL, which §2.8
deliberately removes from Prereg Mode. So: no, and not as a compromise —
the satire targets the behaviour, and the behaviour is absent.

**But the gap is real and should be fixed on its own terms.** Prereg Mode
currently has exactly **one** achievement (`monk`, at 20 prereg days) plus two
shared first-occurrence ones, so a player who adopts the cure sees an empty
ceremony for nineteen days. Measured: the prereg score distribution over 60 days
is `0:13 · 40:9 · 100:32 · 150:6` with a longest run of 4 identical rows — the
mode has almost no texture. It needs its own two or three citations, in Act II's
clinical register, for things preregistration actually makes possible: calling a
null correctly several days running; committing to an analysis on the outcome
that turned out to be the true one; a week without a single significant result.
That is the fix task this question should produce — not a relaxation of the
hacking set.
