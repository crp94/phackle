## 3. Band (a) — the one interpretation call I resolved myself

§3.9(a) "≥99% of null days have ≥30 significant paths" admits two populations. I measured both
and **asserted the post-acceptance one (1.0000, PASS)**, reporting the raw one (0.8220) as a
diagnostic. Reasons, in the file header of `scripts/simulate_calibration.ts` and here:

1. The target's number, 30, is *literally* `NULL_SIG_BAND[0]`. §3.3's rejection sampler exists to
   deliver that floor and is described as "the hackability guarantee".
2. A raw-draw reading would make the sampler's floor redundant — the spec would be building a
   20-attempt rejection loop to enforce something the DGP already satisfied 99% of the time.
3. It explains why the target is **99%, not 100%**: 99% is exactly what a `MAX_ATTEMPTS` fallback
   can cost. Under a raw reading the 99% is unmotivated.
4. It is not a tautology. It fails whenever `MAX_ATTEMPTS` is too small for the band's acceptance
   rate — a live design risk. Here: acceptance 58.4%, so P(exhaust 20) ≈ 1.4e-8, 0 blowouts
   observed in 1,000 days.

The raw figure is the honest statement of how hard the sampler works: **17.8% of unconditioned
null draws are not hackable at all** (p01 = 0 significant paths of 1,792), and 41.6% of attempts
are rejected overall.

By the same rule, **(c) is asserted pre-acceptance**: post-acceptance it is 1.000 *by
construction* (§3.3 conditions on canonical p@400 < .05) and could never land in [0.6, 0.85] under
any setting of any tunable. A band no legal tuning can satisfy is a mis-measurement, not a
mis-tuning. §3.9's own tuning table agrees — it asks for the d range to be calibrated "so
canonical-spec power at N=400 ≈ 0.6–0.8", a property of the d range, not of the accepted sample.

## 4. BLOCKED (d) — §3.9d's literal rule is uninformative under this DGP

### The rule as pinned

"Calls *real* iff the published spec's outcome family holds ≥60% of the day's significant paths",
with the published spec = the first significant spec the greedy explorer finds (controller pin).
Accuracy weighted to the served mix, `0.75·specificity + 0.25·sensitivity` — see §7 for why that
weighting is forced by the spec, not chosen.

### Measured: 0.515 (target [0.75, 0.90])

The landed-family-share distributions for null and effect days are **almost the same
distribution**:

```
  null landed share  : p10=0.197 p25=0.380 p50=0.600 p75=0.844 p90=0.973
  effect landed share: p10=0.151 p25=0.345 p50=0.675 p75=0.880 p90=0.962
```

Threshold sweep (weighted / balanced / sens / spec):

```
    t=0.30  0.323  0.474  0.776  0.172
    t=0.40  0.384  0.496  0.720  0.272
    t=0.50  0.440  0.511  0.654  0.368
    t=0.60  0.515  0.534  0.572  0.496 <-- §3.9d
    t=0.70  0.553  0.529  0.480  0.578
    t=0.80  0.612  0.528  0.360  0.696
    t=0.90  0.655  0.511  0.224  0.798
```

**Best weighted accuracy over all thresholds = 0.655, still below the 0.75 floor. Best balanced
accuracy = 0.534.** The rule is *worse than always saying "noise"* (0.750) at every threshold.
No re-thresholding rescues it, so no constant can either.

### Mechanism (why tuning cannot help)

The rule assumes null-day hits *scatter* across outcome families while effect-day hits *cluster*.
They do not scatter:

```
  null max-family share: p10=0.475 p25=0.538 p50=0.697 p75=0.863 p90=0.973
  null dominant family : Y1..Y4 = 160 / 109 / 134 / 97 days (of 500)
  null mean sig/family : 28.6 / 20.3 / 25.6 / 17.0 (of 448)
```

The four families are *balanced in expectation* (28.6/20.3/25.6/17.0) but *lumpy per draw*: on a
typical null day one family holds ~70% of the hits, and which family it is varies (160/109/134/97
— genuine sampling lumpiness, not a fixed confound). The 448 specs inside a family share one Y
column, so a single lucky sample-level `corr(X, Y_j)` lights up hundreds of paths at once. The
explorer then lands in that family with probability equal to its share (size-biased), so the rule
fires "real" on roughly half of null days by construction.

To make the rule work, the four families would have to light up *together* on null days. That
requires their sample X-Y associations to be strongly positively correlated, which means:

* **Raising `RHO_SHARED`** (0.30). Capped at ~0.375 by the ±25% guard, and effectively capped
  lower by the T3 watch item: excess kurtosis of eps1 scales as
  `(1-ρ)²·6·(5/3)² / [(5/3)(1-ρ)+ρ]²`, i.e. 3.80 at ρ=0.30 → 3.24 at ρ=0.375 (−15%). Measured
  median excess kurtosis(Y1) is already only **1.28** against dgp.test.ts's >1 bar (§6). Even the
  full +25% would move the null max-family share by a few points, not from 0.70 to below 0.60.
* **Raising the shared latent loadings (L1/L4/L6)** to manufacture a universal confound. This is
  self-defeating: a confound strong enough to light all four families on *every* draw would push
  the chance line from "87 of 1,792 (4.9%)" to well over 50%, destroy band (b)'s floor
  (first hit = 1), and gut the pedagogy. And it cannot be made symmetric anyway — **Y4 has no L1
  or L4 term at all**, and adding one changes a PINNED construction.

### The reading that does land in band

§2.7.6 describes the intended teaching signal in different words: *"significance clustering on
the true outcome — robustness across specifications is the real/noise detection skill"*, and
"Null days show the same view: hits scattered **thinly** everywhere." *Thinly* is a statement
about **density within the family**, not about share of the day's hits. Under that reading —
"≥60% of the published spec's own 448-path family is significant" — at the **same pinned 60%
threshold**:

```
    same sweep under the "family density" reading (sigInFamily / 448 >= t):
    t=0.20  0.823  0.811  0.788  0.834
    t=0.30  0.899  0.822  0.668  0.976
    t=0.40  0.889  0.777  0.554  1.000
    t=0.50  0.857  0.714  0.428  1.000
    t=0.60  0.822  0.644  0.288  1.000 <-- at §3.9d's 60%
    t=0.70  0.795  0.591  0.182  1.000
```

**0.822 weighted — inside [0.75, 0.90], margin +0.072 over the floor**, and +7.2 pp of real skill
headroom over the always-"noise" baseline, which is precisely what §3.9's risk table asks the
band to guarantee. (At t=0.30 the same rule reaches 0.899 weighted / 0.822 balanced — genuinely
discriminating rather than baseline-plus-a-bit — but 0.30 is not the pinned threshold.)

### Decision needed

1. **Re-read §3.9d as family *density*** (change one line in the script, no DGP change, no golden
   regeneration) → band (d) passes at 0.822. This is my recommendation: it is textually
   supported by §2.7.6, keeps the pinned 60% threshold, and requires no tuning.
2. Keep the literal rule and accept that §3.9d is unachievable → M1 ships with (d) documented as
   a known deviation.
3. Redesign the DGP so null-day hits genuinely scatter — a PINNED-construction change (Y4 would
   need L1/L4 terms), far outside this task.

## 5. BLOCKED (b) — needs an out-of-guard `NULL_SIG_BAND` change

Median paths-to-first-hit on null days = **15**, target [4, 12]. This tracks the accepted null
sigCount exactly: for a uniformly random permutation with S of M marked, the median first-hit
position is ≈ `ln2·M/S`; at S = 85, that is 14.6. (Confirmed independently: **zero** specs are

`[30, 180]` rejects the entire upper tail (p75 = 170, p90 = 302), so the *accepted* median (85) is

Note the alternative population for (b): over the mix the game actually serves (75% null / 25%

## 7. Definitions I had to pin down (all documented in-script)

| Piece | Definition used | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Day sampling | `seed_i = fnv1a32('cal:'+i)`; type from `dayTypeFor(String(seed_i))`; first 500 of each type; built by the **real** `generatePractice` → the real §3.3 acceptance loop | A suite that re-implemented the acceptance loop would certify nothing. Types are pure hashes, so unneeded days are skipped before any data is generated: exactly 1,000 datasets built. |
| Raw draws | `fnv1a32('calraw:'+i)` with the day's own effect params | Independent stream; deliberately **not** the loop's attempt-0 seed, so this file never hard-codes `day.ts`'s internal seed derivation. |
| Explorer | Seeded Fisher–Yates permutation of all 1,792 specs (mulberry32), walked without replacement, stop at first `valid && p<.05` at **N=200**; position is 1-based; invalid specs count as steps | N=200 is the window every player starts in (§3.8). Invalid specs cost a fork and show a result, so they are steps. (Measured: 0 invalid at N=200 anyway.) |
| Band (b) walks | Exactly **one** walk per day (walk #0), per the pin | — |
| Caller | Published spec = walk #0's first hit; no hit → "noise"; significant paths counted on the same N=200 curve | Controller pin. |
| Accuracy weighting | `(1-pEffect)·specificity + pEffect·sensitivity`, `pEffect = P_EFFECT_PCT/100` | Forced by the spec, not chosen: §3.9's risk table says "base rate 75% 'noise' → always-say-noise = 75% … informed-caller target band (§3.9d) ensures real skill headroom". The band's **floor is exactly the always-say-noise baseline**, which is only true under the served mix. Under a balanced 50/50 reading the 0.75 floor would be an unexplained coincidence. Balanced accuracy is printed too (0.534). |
| p_hit sampling | Nested design: one random permutation per (day, draw); the k-subset is its first k entries | The first k of a uniform random permutation **is** a uniform random k-subset, and it makes `pHit` non-decreasing in k *by construction* — independent per-k subsets can produce `pHit[11] < pHit[10]`, which reads as a broken table in the reveal. |
| p_hit draws/day | **5** (the brief's allowance, "if variance demands") | It does: at 500 days × 1 draw the SE near pHit = 0.5 is `sqrt(.25/500)` = 2.2 pp, and this number is read out loud as a headline percentage ("~52% of the time", §2.7.3). Five draws → 1.0 pp, for 2,500 extra walks over precomputed boolean arrays (milliseconds). Walk #0 is shared with band (b): the two are the same object seen twice, since `P(hit within k) = P(paths-to-first-hit ≤ k)`. |

## 8. `p_hit_by_k.json` and the checksum vector

Written by the script, 500 null days × 5 draws = **2,500 samples per k**, values rounded to 6
decimals. Shipped table (checksum **3889152766**):

```
  k        1     2     3     4     5     7    10    14    20    30    40
  pHit  0.047 0.098 0.143 0.181 0.226 0.297 0.396 0.504 0.621 0.748 0.822
```

Sanity: pHit[14] = 0.504 against §2.7.3's own illustrative copy, *"A researcher exploring 14 paths
at random finds at least one 'significant' result ~52% of the time."* The simulation reproduces the
spec's hand-written example to within 2 pp, which is a good independent check that the DGP, the
grid and the explorer definition are all doing what §3.7 assumed.

**Checksum vector** (`dgpConstantVector()` in `src/engine/reveal.ts`), with an explicit membership
rule so a future editor can tell at a glance whether a new constant belongs: *every exported
constant of `src/engine/dgpConstants.ts`, in that file's declaration order, plus the §3.9 knobs
that change what a "null day" or a "path" even is.* The brief pins the first group's core (R's
rho, all loadings/noise scales, `RHO_SHARED`, treatment coefficients, `EFFECT_D_RANGE`, the HETERO
constants); the covariate constructions are in too, because they decide subgroup membership and
therefore every subgroup spec's sample.

I added **four entries beyond the brief's list**, deliberately, each because it demonstrably
invalidates a table generated before it changed:

* `nullSigBandLo/Hi` — the table is built from **accepted** null days, and this band *is* the
  acceptance filter. It is also the TUNABLE most likely to move (§3.9: "Calibrate via §8.3" — and
  §5 above recommends moving it). Leaving it out would let the single most likely edit ship a
  stale table silently.
* `minCell` — decides which paths are `valid` at all, i.e. which count as hits.
* `pathSpaceSize` — §3.9's tuning table says in as many words: "Path-space size 1,792 — change
  only with p_hit table regen".

Deliberately excluded and documented: `EPOCH`, `P_EFFECT_PCT`, `N_SCHEDULE`, the scoring
constants, `DEBOUNCE_MS`, `MAX_ATTEMPTS`, `TIER_FORKS` — none can change a null day's curve.

**Bootstrap note.** Validation is a function call (`assertPHitTable`, reached from `pHitAtK`), not
a module-load `throw`. A top-level throw would make a stale table *unregenerable*: the script
imports `pHitTableChecksum()` from `reveal.ts` to stamp the file it writes, so it would throw on
the old table before it could write the new one. Failing at first read is early enough — nothing
consumes the table before the reveal.

## 9. `reveal.ts` decisions

* **`RevealMetricsFull`** — `src/engine/types.ts` is not mine to edit, and §6's curve entry has no
  `spec` field, but the controller pinned entries as `{p, explored, published, outcome, spec}`
  (T16's SpecCurve needs `spec` for the recipe callout and tooltips). Resolved by declaring
  `RevealCurveEntry` / `RevealMetricsFull` in `reveal.ts` as a **superset**:
  `RevealCurveEntry[]` stays assignable to §6's entry type, so `RevealMetricsFull` stays
  assignable to `RevealMetrics` and `protocol.ts`'s `RevealPayload` is unaffected. No edit to
  `types.ts`.
* **`totalPaths` = `curve.length` (1,792, invalid points included)** — the denominator §2.7.3's
  copy quotes ("Of 1,792 possible analyses, 87 (4.9%)"), and cross-checked against
  `tests/game/store.test.ts`'s fixture (`totalPaths: 1792, sigPaths: 87, sigFraction: 0.0486` =
  87/1792).
* **Payload `curve` carries VALID points only** (§3.4: sub-MIN_CELL specs are "excluded from the
  curve"). The entry type has no `valid` flag to filter on downstream, so the filtering must
  happen here; T16's "n specs had insufficient data" footnote stays derivable as
  `totalPaths - curve.length`. (Currently always 0 at N=200 — measured.)
* **`explored`/`published` matched by `specKey` value, never object identity** — the store's specs
  are different objects, and a worker `structuredClone` round-trip guarantees they never are the
  same instance.
* **`playerExplored` = `explored.length` verbatim**; the published spec is not implicitly added
  (T12's store owns §2.10 fork accounting).
* **No mutable module state.** The checksum guard recomputes on every `pHitAtK` call (one fnv1a32
  over a ~700-char string, once per reveal) rather than memoising an "already validated" flag —
  the module has no state to get out of sync.
* **`verdictStamp`** resolves the impossible-but-typeable `('effect', spec, null)` case explicitly
  → RETRACTED, rather than leaving it to an `undefined === undefined` accident.
